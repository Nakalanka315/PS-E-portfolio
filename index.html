Job Interviews & Mock Interviews

Preparation Strategy Body Language STAR Method Mock Interviews
Overview

Mastering the interview lifecycle: pre-interview company research, behavioral competency storytelling, active posture, and facing real-time simulated panels.

What I Learned

Researching the hiring organization and market domain is non-negotiable. Body language and vocal tone carry equal weight to verbal answers, and the STAR framework provides structure to situational questions.

Activities Done

Reviewed extensive interview do's and don'ts, submitted personalized CVs and cover letters, and participated in formal mock interview panel evaluations.

The STAR Method Framework

The industry gold standard for answering behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time when..."):

S

Situation

Set the context, company background, and specific scenario clearly in 1–2 crisp sentences.

T

Task

Identify the core challenge, deliverables, and personal responsibility assigned to you.

A

Action

Detail the exact initiatives, leadership steps, and problem-solving techniques you personally applied.

R

Result

Quantify the outcome, metrics achieved, team impact, and key professional lessons gained.

The Complete Interview Protocol

Systematic preparation across every phase of the interview:

01 Before the Interview

  • Research company mission, products & news
  • Dissect job description keywords
  • Practise answers aloud using STAR
  • Prepare 2–3 insightful questions to ask

02 During the Interview

  • Arrive 15 minutes early, groomed & formal
  • Maintain natural eye contact & open posture
  • Pause and listen intently before answering
  • Support answers with real technical examples

03 After the Interview

  • Warmly thank the interview panel
  • Document questions asked & what went well
  • Identify answers that need refinement
  • Send a polite follow-up appreciation email

Key Takeaways

  • 1 Thorough organizational research precedes every other interview activity.
  • 2 Answer behavioral inquiries with verifiable facts using the STAR methodology.
  • 3 Always have thoughtful questions ready when asked "Do you have any questions for us?".
How I Will Apply It: Draft 10 STAR question responses in advance, rehearse aloud in front of a mirror or camera, dress impeccably, and always arrive punctual and composed.

CV Writing & ATS Tailoring

CV Structure Tailoring & ATS Systems Action Verbs Common Mistakes
Overview

Constructing a high-impact, professional Curriculum Vitae (CV) engineered to clear Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) and secure candidate screening interviews.

What I Learned

A CV demands logical reverse-chronological order and precise tailoring with keywords from the job advert. Generic resumes fail automated ATS parsers; impact statements must start with strong action verbs.

Activities Done

Analyzed video case studies on successful CV writing, authored and formatted individual CV submissions, and carried out rigorous peer-review workshops.

What Goes Into a High-Conversion CV

Essential architectural blocks of a modern resume:

a. Personal Information:

Full name, phone, clean professional email, location, LinkedIn link. (No age/photos unless requested).

b. Professional Summary:

A 3-line tailored elevator pitch aligned to the role's primary requirements.

c. Work Experience:

Reverse chronological order with strong action verbs and quantified accomplishments.

d. Education:

Degree, university, expected graduation year, and relevant academic distinctions.

e. Skills Inventory:

Categorized clearly into Technical (languages, tools) and Core Professional skills.

f. Projects & Achievements:

Hackathons, industry workshops, certifications, and portfolio live links.

Tailoring Tips (Pass ATS)

  • Read the job description carefully for exact tool keywords
  • Incorporate job posting keywords into bullet points
  • Highlight the most relevant technical experience first
  • Keep layout clean: standard fonts, 1–2 pages max

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Spelling, syntax, and grammar errors
  • Irrelevant personal hobbies or outdated skills
  • Excessive length or multi-column unparseable tables
  • Inconsistent margins, dates, and font weights

Key Takeaways

  • 1 Tailor the CV specifically for each target role rather than blasting a generic file.
  • 2 Maintain strict reverse chronological ordering across career and education sections.
  • 3 Zero tolerance for typos; ensure unified formatting and clean hierarchy.
How I Will Apply It: Maintain a master CV repository with all experiences, extract a focused 1-page version tailored for each opportunity, and run rigorous proofreading before submitting.

Speaking Skills – Meetings

Meeting Vocabulary Types of Meetings Planning & Agendas Contributing & Roles
Overview

Understanding the language of corporate meetings, meeting archetypes, and how to effectively prepare, chair, and contribute productively to discussions.

What I Learned

Mastery of parliamentary terms such as chair, agenda, minutes, postpone, and bring forward. An effective meeting requires a crisp purpose, distributed agenda, and strict timekeeper.

Activities Done

Participated in a live role-play simulation as the executive committee of a rehabilitation centre, alongside a group worksheet analyzing meeting management dynamics.

Corporate Meeting Vocabulary

Key terms practiced in the practical session:

Set up / Arrange: Organise and schedule a meeting
Bring forward: Move meeting to an earlier date/time
Put back / Postpone: Reschedule meeting to a later date/time
Chair a meeting: Preside over and steer the proceedings
Agenda: The curated list of discussion topics
Minutes: Official written record of decisions made

10-Point Meeting Preparation Checklist:

01. What is the purpose?
02. What outcome do we want?
03. Who should attend?
04. What date and time?
05. Where will it be held?
06. What equipment is needed?
07. How long should it take?
08. Formal or informal format?
09. What papers/docs are needed?
10. What problems could occur?

Key Takeaways

  • 1 Distribute the agenda early so participants arrive informed and prepared.
  • 2 Separate people from problems; critique concepts objectively, not individuals.
  • 3 Appoint a designated timekeeper to keep discussions aligned and actionable.
How I Will Apply It: Circulate a crisp bullet-point agenda prior to any university group sprint and ensure meeting action items and responsibilities are formally logged in written minutes.

Email & Online Etiquette

Email Structure Professional Tone Online Behaviour Digital Footprint
Overview

Establishing rigorous standards for professional email correspondence and understanding how digital etiquette impacts reputation in corporate and academic ecosystems.

What I Learned

Every business email needs a precise subject line, proper salutation, concise purpose stated immediately, polite request, and formal sign-off. Never use all caps, slang, or write emails in an agitated state.

Activities Done

Completed interactive email composition worksheets on realistic scenarios, and successfully reordered a jumbled academic leave request email into standard professional form.

Anatomy of a Professional Email

Interactive breakdown of the classroom exemplar email:

To: lecturer@university.lk
Subject: Request for a meeting about my assignment
1. Proper Greeting: "Dear Ms. Weerasinghe," (Respectful title and surname; never casual 'Hey').

2. Purpose First: I am writing to ask whether it would be possible to meet this week to discuss my assignment.

3. Polite Request & Options: I am free on Tuesday or Thursday afternoon. Thank you for your time.

4. Sign-off & Signature:
Kind regards,
R.M.P.N Akalanaka Wijekoon
SA25610214

Do's

  • Use a formal, branded university or professional email
  • Keep paragraphs concise and scannable
  • Check spelling, syntax, and attachments before sending
  • Use courteous phrases: "Would it be possible..."

Don'ts

  • Never write sentences in ALL CAPS (shouting)
  • Avoid emojis, SMS abbreviations, or slang ("LOL", "thx")
  • Never compose or hit send during emotional conflict
  • Refrain from forwarding spam or unverified chains

Key Takeaways

  • 1 A clear, descriptive subject line sets professional credibility immediately.
  • 2 Frame communications as polite requests offering flexible options, not rigid demands.
  • 3 Sleep on high-stress replies: never send an angry response in real-time.
How I Will Apply It: Always use my formal student email address, re-read drafts for tone and clarity, and ensure full contact signatures are included in all external correspondence.

Telephone Etiquette & Customer Service

Answering Calls Hold & Transfer Protocols De-escalating Upset Callers Voice & Pitch Control
Overview

Mastering phone interactions, managing customer expectations, transferring calls gracefully, and de-escalating frustrated stakeholders.

What I Learned

Pick up within three rings, state your identity and department clearly, repeat vital figures, and ask consent before putting someone on hold. Physical smiling noticeably enriches vocal warmth.

Activities Done

Reviewed customer service training video modules, answered workbook call simulations, and completed active phone-call listening assessments.

Step-by-Step Anatomy of a Professional Call

Standard operating procedure for telephonic communications:

1. Answer Promptly:

Pick up within three rings with an intentional smile to set a positive vocal tone.

2. Introduce Yourself:

"Good morning, [Department], this is [Name], how may I assist you today?"

3. Give Full Attention:

Eliminate side conversations, typing noise, or multitasking while caller speaks.

4. Take Notes & Repeat:

Transcribe vital numbers, names, and requests, then read back to confirm accuracy.

5. Avoid Bad Habits:

Never eat, chew gum, sip liquids, or shuffle background papers over the receiver.

6. Solve or Escalate:

Keep holds under 60 seconds; provide clear solutions or routed transfers.

Putting Someone on Hold

  • Always ask permission first: "May I place you on a brief hold?"
  • Wait for their affirmative response before pressing hold
  • State the reason (e.g. checking system records)
  • Thank them sincerely upon reconnecting

Handling an Upset Caller

  • Remain calm and remember frustration is not personal
  • Demonstrate active empathy: listen without interrupting
  • Never argue, apportion blame, or make excuses
  • Commit to a concrete resolution and agreed follow-up timeline

Key Takeaways

  • 1 Answer incoming calls within three rings to convey responsiveness.
  • 2 Always ask permission before placing a caller on hold or transferring.
  • 3 Let the caller hang up first to prevent abrupt disconnection.
How I Will Apply It: Introduce myself professionally on all calls, log instructions systematically on a notepad, and confirm next steps before concluding conversations.
Further Reading: Delivering Knock Your Socks Off Service — Kristin Anderson & Ron Zemke
Pragmatic concepts for delivering outstanding customer service across phone, text, and in-person touchpoints.

Emotional Intelligence (EQ)

What Emotions Are Functions of Emotions EQ vs IQ Self-Regulation
Overview

Understanding the biological basis of emotions, why we experience them, and how emotional maturity fosters effective leadership and workplace collaboration.

What I Learned

An emotion consists of a subjective feeling, physiological body reaction, and expressive behavior. EQ involves recognizing and regulating emotional states within oneself and peers.

Activities Done

Deconstructed real-world workplace conflict scenarios, analyzed micro-expressions on facial features, and performed self-scoring emotional resilience tests.

The Structure of Emotional Intelligence

Daniel Goleman's five fundamental dimensions of emotional intelligence:

01
Self-Awareness

Knowing what you feel and how it influences actions.

02
Self-Regulation

Channeling disruptive emotions and pausing before reacting.

03
Motivation

Internal drive to achieve beyond financial reward.

04
Empathy

Sensing others' feelings and taking an active interest.

05
Social Skills

Managing relationships and inspiring team consensus.

IQ (Intellectual Quotient)

  • Logic, syntax, abstract analysis, and mathematical reasoning
  • Technical and domain-specific know-how
  • Helps candidates clear benchmark tests and get hired

EQ (Emotional Quotient)

  • Understanding complex feelings, empathy, and social dynamics
  • Can be intentionally cultivated and refined throughout lifetime
  • Empowers individuals to thrive as leaders and managers

Key Takeaways

  • 1 Always pause before reacting to stressful situations or critical remarks.
  • 2 Emotions represent valuable physiological data, not enemies to be suppressed.
  • 3 Emotional intelligence is an adaptable skill that can be consistently sharpened.
How I Will Apply It: Take a deliberate three-second breath when experiencing stress in project deadlines, and practice active listening without formulating immediate rebuttals.
Further Reading: Emotional Intelligence — Daniel Goleman
The groundbreaking work examining why EQ determines professional efficacy and leadership success more than IQ alone.

Dining Etiquette & Table Manners

Place Settings Cutlery Sequence Napkin Etiquette Corporate Dinner Manners
Overview

Conducting oneself with refinement during formal corporate dinners, diplomatic banquets, and business networking meals.

What I Learned

Work cutlery from the outside in; remember bread plate on the left and drink glasses on the right (the 'b' and 'd' hand mnemonic); napkin remains unfolded on lap; salt and pepper are always passed together.

Activities Done

Studied multi-course formal place settings diagrams, engaged in interactive role-plays, and took an intensive True/False dining etiquette quiz.

Formal Place Setting & The "b" & "d" Rule

Never pick up the wrong bread roll or drink again:

b
Left Hand = "b" for Bread

Make an 'OK' sign with your left hand forming the letter 'b'. Your bread plate and butter knife are always on your left.

d
Right Hand = "d" for Drink

Make an 'OK' sign with your right hand forming the letter 'd'. Your water goblet and drink glasses are always on your right.

1. Outside In:

Start each course with the outermost utensils and work inward toward the plate.

2. Napkin on Lap:

Wait for the host, then gently unfold and drape the napkin across your lap.

3. Break the Bread:

Never cut rolls with a knife; tear off one bite-sized piece at a time with fingers.

4. Salt & Pepper Together:

They are lifelong partners; always pass both shakers together even if one was requested.

5. Mobile Phone Away:

Keep devices off the table and on silent to give full attention to guests.

6. Finished Position:

Place knife and fork parallel diagonally across plate pointing toward 10 and 4 o'clock.

Key Takeaways

  • 1 Use cutlery methodically from the outermost pieces working inward.
  • 2 Bread belongs on your left, drinks on your right (remember 'b' & 'd').
  • 3 True manners are about putting table companions at complete ease.
How I Will Apply It: Apply these etiquette guidelines at university alumni dinners, formal dinners, and professional receptions, ensuring my phone remains out of sight.
Further Reading: The Rituals of Dinner — Margaret Visser
An anthropological exploration into the historical origins and social power of dining table etiquette.
